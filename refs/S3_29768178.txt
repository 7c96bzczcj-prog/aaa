29768178 2019 01 28 2019 01 28 1097-4180 48 5 2018 May 15 Immunity Immunity Trained Memory of Human Uterine NK Cells Enhances Their Function in Subsequent Pregnancies. 951 962.e5 951-962.e5 10.1016/j.immuni.2018.03.030 S1074-7613(18)30128-6 Natural killer cells (NKs) are abundant in the human decidua, regulating trophoblast invasion and angiogenesis. Several diseases of poor placental development are associated with first pregnancies, so we thus looked to characterize differences in decidual NKs (dNKs) in first versus repeated pregnancies. We discovered a population found in repeated pregnancies, which has a unique transcriptome and epigenetic signature, and is characterized by high expression of the receptors NKG2C and LILRB1. We named these cells Pregnancy Trained decidual NK cells (PTdNKs). PTdNKs have open chromatin around the enhancers of IFNG and VEGFA. Activation&#xa0;of PTdNKs led to increased production and secretion of IFN-&#x3b3; and VEGF&#x3b1;, with the latter supporting vascular sprouting and tumor growth. The precursors of PTdNKs seem to be found in the&#xa0;endometrium. Because repeated pregnancies are associated with improved placentation, we propose that PTdNKs, which are present primarily in repeated pregnancies, might be involved in proper placentation.
