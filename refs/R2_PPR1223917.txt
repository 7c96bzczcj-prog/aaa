Transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) is a central suppressor of natural killer (NK) cell function in tissues and tumor microenvironments, yet it remains unclear whether NK cell suppression requires continuous TGF-β signaling or can become stably imprinted. Here, we dissect the outcome of TGF-β signaling on NK cell transcriptional, epigenetic, and functional programs in short-term vs. long-term stimulation. We show that short-term TGF-β exposure induces limited and largely reversible transcriptional and chromatin accessibility changes, resulting in transient impairment of NK cell effector function. In contrast, prolonged TGF-β signalling drives durable epigenetic remodelling accompanied by sustained suppression of cytotoxicity and cytokine production that persists even after signal withdrawal, indicating that NK cell dysfunction becomes independent of continued TGF-β exposure. Prolonged TGF-β exposure is associated with loss of chromatin accessibility at regulatory elements controlling NK cell effector programs. These persistently less accessible regions are enriched for binding motifs of interferon regulatory factors (IRFs) as well as the lineage-defining transcription factors EOMES and T-BET, providing a mechanistic basis for impaired cytokine responsiveness. Reduced H3K4me3 occupancy at selected effector gene promoters further supports a repressive chromatin state. In contrast to the lasting repression of effector genes, genes associated with tissue residency exhibit largely reversible transcriptional and epigenetic regulation, revealing distinct regulatory modes governing effector function versus tissue adaptation. NK cells from patients with hepatocellular carcinoma display impaired effector function and epigenetic alterations overlapping with features induced by prolonged TGF-β exposure, consistent with chronic signaling in vivo . Together, our findings reveal persistent TGF-β-driven epigenetic remodelling as a central mechanism of NK cell dysfunction in cancer and identify signal duration as a key determinant of NK cell fate, with implications for therapeutic strategies aimed at restoring NK cell function.

## Introduction
 Natural killer (NK) cells are pivotal players in anti-tumor immunity due to their unique ability to rapidly recognize and eliminate target cells in an antigen-independent manner . Beyond direct cytotoxicity, NK cells secrete proinflammatory cytokines and chemokines that shape adaptive immune responses, positioning them as powerful effectors in cancer immunotherapy , , , . However, despite growing interest in NK cell-based therapeutic strategies, their efficacy in solid tumors remains limited, largely due to potent immunosuppressive cues within the tumor microenvironment. , , . NK cell activity is finely tuned through a balance of activating and inhibitory signals, which converge on transcriptional and epigenetic regulatory programs to shape functional output , . Among these inhibitory cues in the tumor microenvironment, transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) stands out as a major functional suppressor, dampening NK cell cytotoxicity, cytokine production and cellular metabolism , , , , , , . In addition, long-term TGF-β signaling leads to acquisition of tissue residency (TR) markers on NK cells, driving NK cells towards a type 1 innate lymphoid cell (ILC1)-like phenotype associated with reduced anti-tumor functionality .
 Canonical TGF-β signaling is mediated by SMAD transcription factors (TFs). Upon ligand binding, TGF-β receptor II phosphorylates SMAD2 and SMAD3, which then form heterotrimeric complexes with SMAD4. These complexes translocate to the nucleus where they associate with partner TFs to bind target gene loci and recruit transcriptional co-regulators thereby shaping transcriptional output . These dynamic transcriptional interactions render TGF-β signaling output highly context-specific. In parallel, non-canonical TGF-β signaling pathways contribute to NK cell plasticity, including the induction of TR programs . Together, these pathways suggest that TGF-β signaling output is highly dynamic and sensitive to cellular context. However, the mechanisms that distinguish transient from long-lasting functional consequences of TGF-β signaling in NK cells remain poorly defined.
 Cytokine signals regulate NK cell function through a complex network of transcriptional and epigenetic networks, some of which drive lasting functional reprogramming , , , , . For TGF-β in particular, signal duration has been proposed as a critical determinant of downstream outcome , , . Despite this, a systematic analysis of how short-term versus prolonged exposure shapes NK cell epigenetic landscapes is lacking. Moreover, while epigenetic regulation is a hallmark of TGF-β responses in other cell types, including T cells , , , , , , the global epigenetic programs induced by TGF-β in NK cells remain incompletely characterized .
 Here, we tested the hypothesis that TGF-β signal duration determines whether NK cell suppression remains reversible or becomes stably imprinted. We demonstrate that short-term TGF-β exposure induces largely reversible transcriptional and epigenetic changes, whereas prolonged signaling enforces a persistent dysfunctional state that fails to recover after signal withdrawal. This durable dysfunction is accompanied by stable epigenetic remodeling at regulatory elements controlling NK cell effector programs, while TR-associated genes remain largely reversible. Together, these findings establish signal duration as a key determinant of NK cell fate and demonstrate that chronic TGF-β signaling imprints a persistent epigenetic state that sustains NK cell dysfunction independently of continued TGF-β exposure.

## Results and discussion

## TGF-β signal duration governs transcriptional and epigenetic outcomes
 Depending on the duration of signaling, TGF-β can drive NK cells toward dysfunction, metabolic reprogramming, or conversion into ILC1-like cells , , , . However, whether short and prolonged TGF-β exposure engage distinct gene regulatory programs that underlie divergent functional outcomes in NK cells remains unresolved. To address this, we conducted RNA-sequencing (RNA-seq) and assay for transposase-accessible chromatin and sequencing (ATAC-seq) on sorted human NK cells (CD3 - CD14 - CD19 - CD56 + ) following short-term (3 hours, a time point at which canonical SMAD2/3 phosphorylation is already robustly induced) or prolonged (7 days) TGF-β exposure ( ). After 3 hours of TGF-β exposure, we observed only modest transcriptional and epigenetic changes, with 427 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and 2,314 differentially accessible regions (DARs) ( , ). By contrast, prolonged stimulation resulted in extensive transcriptional and even more pronounced epigenetic reprogramming, yielding 966 DEGs and 26,538 DARs ( , ). Short-term TGF-β primarily induced rapid upregulation of inhibitory regulators of its own signalling ( SMAD7, SMURF2, SKI) ( ). In contrast, genes differentially regulated after 7-day TGF-β treatment were associated with TR (upregulation of CD9, ITGA1, ITGAE, ZNF683 downregulation of KLF2 ) and effector dampening (downregulation of IFNG, GZMA, TBX21, CD226 ) ( ).
 Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) of DEGs in short-term TGF-β treated NK cells revealed exclusive enrichment of upregulated pathways, including epithelial-mesenchymal transition and TGF-β signaling. In contrast, prolonged TGF-β stimulation led predominantly to downregulation of multiple pathways, most notably oxidative phosphorylation, interferon (IFN)-γ and IFN-α response and MYC targets ( ). This data highlights a duration-dependent shift in the transcriptional consequence of TGF-β signaling.
 Consistent with this, comparison of DEGs across time points revealed limited overlap between short- and long-term TGF-β responses, with only 47 genes regulated under both conditions ( ). Notably, this shared gene set was enriched for negative regulators of TGF-β signaling, including SKI, SMAD7 , and SMURF2 , suggesting that engagement of feedback control represents a conserved transcriptional response independent of signal duration.
 We next compared global chromatin accessibility profiles under brief versus prolonged TGF-β stimulation. Here, a majority of genes associated with short-term DARs overlapped with those associated with long-term DARs ( ), suggesting that epigenetic programs initiated by short-term TGF-β stimulation are maintained during prolonged exposure. DARs in both stimulation conditions were enriched in intergenic and intronic putative enhancer regions with relatively few promoter-associated regions affected ( ). Importantly, the directionality of accessibility changes differed markedly between time points: short-term TGF-β exposure was associated primarily with increased accessibility at regulatory regions, whereas prolonged exposure resulted in reduced accessibility at approximately half of all DARs ( ). Notably, even brief (3 h) TGF-β exposure resulted in decreased accessibility at key effector loci, including IFNG, GZMA , and GZMB in a subset of donors ( ), indicating that epigenetic regulation at critical effector genes is initiated early during TGF-β signaling. Together, these findings reveal that prolonged TGF-β exposure induces a transcriptional program largely distinct from that triggered by short-term stimulation, highlighting signal duration as a critical variable in shaping NK cell gene regulation and raising the question of how this impacts NK cell function.

## TGF-β signal duration shapes the extent and persistence of NK cell dysfunction
 TGF-β can inhibit key NK cell functions including cytotoxicity and production of key NK cell effector cytokines such as IFN-γ , . We therefore asked to which extent the distinct transcriptional and epigenetic remodeling patterns induced by short-term versus long-term TGF-β exposure are linked to suppression of these NK cell functions. To address this, we exposed NK cells to TGF-β for 3 h or 7 days, and first determined the capability of these cells to kill BxPC3 pancreatic cancer cells. Untreated control NK cells already displayed robust cytotoxic activity after 3 h of culture, and this activity was not significantly reduced by short-term TGF-β exposure, indicating that brief signaling is insufficient to suppress NK cell cytotoxicity ( ). In contrast, NK cells cultured for 7 days retained strong cytotoxic capacity under control conditions but exhibited profound functional inhibition following prolonged TGF-β treatment (( ). To define the temporal requirements for functional suppression, we next analyzed cytotoxicity after 24 h of TGF-β exposure and observed significant inhibition comparable to that seen after 7 days ( ). Together, these findings indicate that TGF-β-mediated suppression of NK cell cytotoxicity is not immediate but requires crossing a temporal threshold.
 Having defined a temporal threshold for the onset of TGF-β–mediated functional suppression, we next asked whether transient exposure is sufficient to induce lasting phenotypic changes or whether continued signaling is required to maintain them. To this end, NK cells were stimulated with TGF-β for 3 h or 24 h, followed by washout and a 6-day resting period to assess the stability of functional changes induced by transient TGF-β signalling. These conditions were compared to NK cells continuously exposed to TGF-β for 7 days as a model of chronic signaling. Prolonged TGF-β exposure induced robust changes in NK cell phenotype, including strong upregulation of TR-associated surface markers such as CD103, CD9, and CD49a, whereas 3 h or 24 h exposure resulted in only minimal and transient changes, indicating that stable acquisition of TR features requires sustained TGF-β signaling ( , ).
 In contrast to TR-associated phenotypes, analysis of effector function revealed a graded and more persistent impairment that scaled with signal duration. Consistent with this pattern, IFN-γ and granzyme A and B expression were markedly reduced after 7 days of continuous TGF-β exposure ( , ). Notably, even 3 h of TGF-β stimulation resulted in a slight but detectable reduction in granzyme levels after six days of rest, an effect that was further enhanced following 24 h exposure ( , ). Thus, transient TGF-β signaling was sufficient to initiate lasting functional impairment, although to a lesser extent than sustained exposure In line with this, IL-12/IL-18 re-stimulation on day 7 revealed a clear hierarchy of dysfunction: mild suppression after 3 h exposure followed by rest, more pronounced inhibition after 24 h exposure followed by rest, and the strongest loss of IFN-γ production and granzyme expression after 7 days of continuous TGF-β treatment ( ).
 Collectively, these findings define a temporal threshold for NK cell suppression and show that, once this threshold is crossed, even transient TGF-β exposure can initiate dysfunction that partially persists after withdrawal, raising the possibility that early signaling events begin to reprogram NK cell fate.

## Durability of long-term TGF-β effects diverges between NK cell functional programs
 Having observed that transient TGF-β exposure can initiate partially persistent dysfunction, we next asked whether prolonged signaling consolidates these changes into a stable and long-lasting dysfunctional state. To address this, NK cells were stimulated with TGF-β for 7 days, followed by a 10-day resting period, and subsequently analyzed for effector function, metabolic fitness, and expression of TR-associated markers ( ). Sustained TGF-β exposure (17 days) robustly induced TR-associated markers CD103, CD9, and CD49a, while reducing intracellular granzyme A, granzyme B, and IFN-γ levels ( ). Following TGF-β withdrawal for 10 days, TR markers largely returned to baseline, underscoring the requirement for continuous TGF-β signaling to maintain this phenotype ( ).
 To exclude the possibility that loss of TR features reflected selective expansion of TR-negative cells, CD103 + and CD103 − NK cells were sorted after 7 days of TGF-β exposure and cultured separately ( ). Both populations showed comparable loss of CD103 expression following TGF-β withdrawal ( ), demonstrating that even CD103 + NK cells require sustained signaling to maintain TR marker expression. Notably, effector function did not differ between CD103 + and CD103 − NK cells at day 17, with CD103 − cells displaying comparable or even lower IFN-γ expression ( ), further supporting a dissociation between TR features and effector dysfunction.
 In contrast to the reversibility of TR features, NK cell effector function demonstrated sustained impairment after TGF-β withdrawal. Reduced expression of granzyme A and B and IFN-γ was maintained even after 10 days of rest ( ), indicating long-lasting functional suppression independent of ongoing signaling. This impairment was not attributable to metabolic defects, as mitochondrial mass and mitochondrial membrane potential were comparable between TGF-β-exposed and control NK cells at day 17 ( ).
 Consistent with these findings, RNA-seq analysis across all time points revealed that TR-associated transcripts ( ITGAE, ZNF683, ITGA1, ITGB4, CD9 ) were selectively upregulated only under continuous TGF-β exposure, but returned to baseline after withdrawal ( ). In contrast, effector genes ( GZMA, GZMB, PRF1, LAMP1, IFNG ) remained persistently downregulated even after prolonged signal withdrawal ( ). NK cell proliferation was likewise durably impaired, as reflected by reduced Ki67 expression ( ).
 To determine whether this defect reflected disruption of specific activation pathways, TGF-β– naïve and TGF-β-exposed NK cells (with or without recovery) were re-stimulated using diverse activating cues, including IFN-α, IL-2 plus IL-15, IL-12 plus IL-18, and activating receptor ligation. Intriguingly, none of these stimuli fully restored NK cell effector function ( , ), indicating a broad dysfunction program spanning multiple activating pathways. Consistent with this observation, GSEA revealed a shared set of pathways that remained downregulated across all prolonged exposure conditions, including inflammatory responses and IFN-α and IFN-γ signaling ( ). These pathways encompassed key transcriptional regulators such as STAT1, IRF1, IRF7 , and IRF8 , along with effector-associated genes like GZMA , and IL18R1 ( ). Together, these findings reveal a fundamental divergence in the durability of TGF-β-induced NK cell programs, with TR-associated features remaining dependent on continuous signaling, while effector dysfunction persists independently of signal withdrawal.

## Functional impairment after prolonged TGF-β exposure is accompanied by persistent epigenetic reprogramming
 Given the durable functional impairment observed after prolonged TGF-β exposure, we next asked whether stable epigenetic programs underlie the persistent NK cell phenotype. To test this, we performed RNA-seq and ATAC-seq on day 17, comparing TGF-β-naïve NK cells, NK cells continuously exposed to TGF-β for 17 days, and NK cells exposed for 7 days followed by 10 days of signal withdrawal. To focus specifically on TGF-β-driven effects, we restricted our analysis to genes whose regulation at day 7 was attributable to TGF-β rather than IL-15 ( ).
 Within this TGF-β-regulated gene set, 1,069 DEGs were identified in continuously treated NK cells, while 228 DEGs remained differentially expressed after TGF-β withdrawal, compared to controls ( ). Temporal clustering of these genes revealed four distinct expression patterns: transiently upregulated, transiently downregulated, persistently upregulated, and persistently downregulated transcripts ( ). Transiently upregulated genes that returned to baseline after withdrawal included TIMP1 and CTLA4 , whereas persistently upregulated genes included SMAD3 . In contrast, transiently downregulated genes included KLF2 , consistent with the reversibility of TR features, while persistently downregulated genes comprised key effector molecules such as GZMB, PRF1 , and LAMP1 ( ), in line with sustained functional impairment.
 Epigenetic profiling revealed extensive chromatin remodeling in both continuously TGF-β–treated and TGF-β-withdrawn NK cells, with 27,798 DARs detected in continuously treated NK cells and 9,182 DARs persisting after TGF-β withdrawal ( ). Notably, the majority of DARs observed after TGF-β withdrawal overlapped with those detected after 17 days of continuous exposure, indicating persistence of TGF-β-induced epigenetic remodeling rather than acquisition of new chromatin states upon signal withdrawal ( ). Consistent with transcriptional patterns, chromatin accessibility changes segregated into four temporal clusters, mirroring transient versus persistent regulation ( ). Importantly, approximately one quarter of DARs detected at day 7 remained altered after TGF-β withdrawal, with the majority exhibiting sustained loss of accessibility. These persistently less accessible regions mapped to genes critical for NK cell effector function and downstream signaling, including IL12RB, GZMB, IFNG, NFKB , and IRF2 ( ). Additional transcription start site (TSS)-focused analysis demonstrated sustained loss of accessibility at selected effector loci after TGF-β withdrawal, notably GZMA, IFNG, GZMM , and PRF1 , consistent with a TGF-β-driven mechanism that locks specific TSSs in a less accessible state ( ). These results reveal that prolonged TGF-β exposure imprints durable epigenetic programs in NK cells characterized by selective and persistent repression of effector-associated regulatory elements, thereby providing a molecular basis for sustained functional impairment after signal withdrawal.

## Prolonged TGF-β exposure selectively restricts IRF-, EOMES-, and T-BET-associated regulatory elements
 To elucidate the mechanisms underlying durable epigenetic repression at some loci but not others, we performed TF motif analysis across the distinct ATAC-seq accessibility clusters. Regions exhibiting sustained loss of accessibility after prolonged TGF-β exposure were strongly enriched for binding motifs of IRFs as well as the lineage-defining TFs EOMES and T-BET ( ), indicating preferential repression of regulatory elements central to NK cell identity and effector function upon prolonged TGF-β exposure , , , , . In contrast, regions that gained accessibility in a sustained manner were enriched for CTCF and BORIS motifs, suggesting durable remodeling of higher-order chromatin organization , . Regions that were transiently less accessible at day 7 but recovered after signal withdrawal were enriched for NF-κB family motifs ( ), consistent with reversible suppression of activation-associated transcriptional programs .
 To assess the functional relevance of these motif enrichments, we examined DARs at key effector gene loci. DARs linked to IFNG, GZMA and GZMB contained binding sites for IRF1, IRF8, EOMES, and T-BET, and exhibited sustained loss of accessibility following prolonged TGF-β exposure ( , ). IRF family members are central integrators of cytokine signaling downstream of IL-12, IL-18, and type I interferons , , , , while EOMES and T-BET are critical for NK cell lineage commitment and effector function , , , , , , . Persistent restriction of these regulatory elements therefore provides a mechanistic basis for persistent functional impairment of NK cell activation and the failure to fully restore function upon re-stimulation.
 We next inferred TF activity based on target gene expression changes using univariate linear models (ULM) and the DoRothEA network across treatment conditions , . Activity of multiple IRF family members (IRF1, IRF2, IRF3, IRF4, IRF7, IRF8), as well as EOMES and T-BET, was significantly reduced after 7 days of TGF-β exposure ( ). Although partial recovery of several factors was observed by day 17, including after TGF-β withdrawal, none returned to baseline activity levels; IRF1 and IRF2 activity remained significantly suppressed across both continuous exposure and withdrawal conditions ( ). Analysis of transcript abundance revealed discordance between TF expression and inferred activity. While IRF1, IRF8 , and TBX21 transcript levels mirrored the sustained reduction in activity, EOMES expression remained largely unchanged over time ( ), indicating that impaired TF activity is not solely explained by transcriptional downregulation. Similarly, transcript levels of IRF2, IRF3, IRF4 , and IRF7 did not uniformly decrease following prolonged TGF-β exposure ( ), further supporting epigenetic rather than transcriptional mechanisms of regulation. Beyond these factors, we also observed persistently reduced activity of STAT1, STAT3, STAT4, STAT5A, and STAT5B at day 17, irrespective of signal withdrawal ( ), revealing an additional layer of dysregulation not captured by motif enrichment.
 Taken together, these findings indicate that prolonged TGF-β exposure selectively restricts chromatin accessibility and activity of TFs that integrate cytokine signaling and define NK cell effector identity, thereby providing a mechanistic basis for durable multi-pathway NK cell dysfunction.

## Prolonged TGF-β signaling reshapes promoter states
 To further define how prolonged TGF-β exposure alters transcriptional output, we examined global changes in promoter-associated chromatin features and chromatin architecture by profiling the initiation-associated histone mark H3K4me3 and the architectural protein CTCF via CUT&RUN , . Genome-wide analysis revealed that prolonged TGF-β signaling was associated with a modest but consistent reduction of H3K4me3 at promoter regions, whereas acute stimulation induced a transient increase in H3K4me3 signal ( ). These findings indicate that sustained, but not brief, TGF-β exposure is associated with impaired transcriptional initiation at promoters, reinforcing the notion that signal duration drives qualitatively distinct molecular programs.
 In parallel, CTCF abundance was globally increased following prolonged TGF-β exposure ( ). CTCF is a key architectural protein involved in chromatin looping and regulation of enhancer-promoter interactions , , , . While increased CTCF occupancy was not preferentially localized to effector or TR loci, its global enrichment is consistent with broader architectural adaptation rather than locus-restricted effects , , , .
 Inspection of representative gene loci revealed distinct promoter-associated chromatin configurations associated with divergent transcriptional outcomes. TR-associated genes, which were transcriptionally induced but not stably repressed, exhibited increased H3K4me3 at promoter regions, consistent with continued transcriptional competence ( ). In contrast, effector genes that underwent persistent transcriptional repression displayed reduced promoter-associated H3K4me3, indicative of initiation-impaired promoter states ( ). These findings support a model whereby prolonged TGF-β signaling remodels promoter chromatin states and reshapes the TF environment to selectively constrain effector programs. Of note, consistent with the use of primary human NK cells, CUT&RUN signals exhibited inter-donor variability at individual loci. However, global patterns of promoter-associated H3K4me3 reduction and increased CTCF occupancy were observed across donors, supporting the robustness of the observed chromatin remodeling.
 Collectively, these results indicate that sustained TGF-β signaling imposes durable changes in promoter-associated chromatin states and global chromatin architecture that selectively limit transcriptional initiation at effector gene loci.

## NK cells from patients with liver tumors exhibit epigenetic and functional features of prolonged TGF-β signaling
 One physiological context in which NK cells are likely to experience prolonged TGF-β exposure is the tumor microenvironment . To assess whether the chromatin and transcriptional programs defined under sustained TGF-β exposure are reflected in human disease, we analyzed NK cell phenotype, function and chromatin accessibility in patients with primary liver cancer or liver metastases who were not undergoing immunotherapy ( ). Tumor-infiltrating NK cells from these patients displayed significantly increased expression of CD103 ( ), consistent with ongoing TGF-β signaling within tumors. In parallel, peripheral blood NK cells from patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) showed reduced IFN-γ production and diminished degranulation upon re-stimulation, as measured by CD107a expression ( ), indicating sustained functional suppression beyond the tumor site.
 To determine whether this functional impairment was accompanied by stable epigenetic changes, we performed ATAC-seq on peripheral blood NK cells from patients with HCC and healthy controls. Chromatin accessibility profiles clearly distinguished patient-derived NK cells from healthy controls ( ) and revealed a widespread loss of chromatin accessibility in HCC-associated NK cells ( ), supporting the presence of disease-associated epigenetic repression. ( ). Examination of loci associated with NK cell effector function revealed reduced chromatin accessibility at upstream regulatory regions of IFNG and GZMA containing putative T-bet and IRF1 binding sites ( , ), as well as decreased accessibility at the IRF8 locus ( ). These alterations overlapped with key features of the chromatin changes induced by prolonged TGF-β exposure in vitro , supporting the relevance of this epigenetic program in vivo . At the same time, additional disease-associated accessibility alterations were observed that did not fully recapitulate the TGF-β-driven program, consistent with the presence of multiple suppressive signals within the tumor environment.
 Collectively, these data demonstrate that NK cells from patients with HCC exhibit functional impairment and stable chromatin accessibility changes that recapitulate features of prolonged TGF-β exposure, positioning chronic TGF-β signaling as a central driver of epigenetic NK cell dysfunction in human tumors.

## Discussion
 TGF-β is a major suppressive signal in tissues and tumor microenvironments and TGF-β blockade is actively pursued as a therapeutic strategy in cancer, based on the premise that suppressive signaling restrains immune cell function in a reversible manner . Yet with regard to NK cells, most studies have focused on the acute effects of TGF-β, leaving unresolved whether established NK cell dysfunction depends on continued signaling or persists after signal withdrawal , , , , . This distinction is critical: if chronic TGF-β exposure imprints a stable dysfunctional state, late therapeutic intervention may fail to restore NK cell activity. Here, we demonstrate that prolonged TGF-β signaling enforces durable epigenetic remodeling and sustained effector suppression, whereas short-term exposure induces largely reversible changes, revealing that the reversibility of NK cell dysfunction is fundamentally determined by the duration of signaling. Importantly, the chronically imprinted state induced by prolonged TGF-β signaling is refractory to diverse reactivation cues, including pro-inflammatory cytokines and activating receptor engagement, indicating a loss of functional plasticity rather than simple signal-dependent suppression. Together, these data suggest a mechanistic concept whereby acute TGF-β signaling in NK cells enables flexible but transient immune adaptation, whereas chronic exposure promotes epigenetically stabilized terminal dysfunction. This temporal framework may extend to other cytokines and settings of chronic immune suppression, including cancer and persistent inflammation.
 A central mechanistic insight of our study is that prolonged TGF-β exposure does not uniformly remodel the NK cell epigenome, but instead selectively targets distinct regulatory modules. While the majority of TGF-β-responsive genes, including TR-associated genes, recover chromatin accessibility and transcription upon signal withdrawal, a substantial subset of loci remains epigenetically altered. Notably, regions that fail to regain accessibility are highly enriched for binding motifs of IRFs as well as the lineage-defining TFs EOMES and T-BET, which together integrate cytokine signaling and maintain NK cell effector identity , , , , , , , . Persistent closure of IRF-, EOMES-, and T-BET-associated regulatory elements at promoters and enhancers therefore provides a mechanistic basis for the durable repression of effector genes and the failure of NK cells to respond to multiple activating stimuli after chronic TGF-β exposure.
 At the chromatin level, this selective vulnerability of effector-associated regulatory elements is mirrored by corresponding changes in promoter-associated histone modifications and higher-order chromatin organization. Effector gene promoters display a loss of the permissive histone mark H3K4me3 , in line with durable transcriptional repression, whereas promoters of TR-associated genes maintain or gain H3K4me3. This dichotomy further distinguishes epigenetically stabilized effector repression from transcriptionally labile tissue adaptation programs. The global increase in CTCF occupancy observed after prolonged TGF-β exposure suggests that higher-order chromatin organization may contribute to stabilizing effector repression, although the precise architectural mechanisms remain to be defined , .
 Our findings are consistent with extensive evidence that prolonged TGF-β signaling contributes to chronic lymphocyte dysfunction in settings of persistent infection and cancer. In T cells, sustained TGF-β exposure has been linked to exhausted or attenuated differentiation states that limit effector function and cytokine responsiveness , , . In several settings, these programs remain at least partly reversible as genetic or pharmacologic interference with TGF-β signaling can restore effector function, enhance survival, or improve responsiveness to checkpoint blockade, consistent with continued dependence on active pathway engagement rather than fully fixed epigenetic commitment , , , , . In contrast, our data indicate that extended TGF-β exposure can impose a more durable form of functional impairment in NK cells, as withdrawal of TGF-β alone is insufficient to restore effector competence. Importantly, TGF-β-driven TR features remain dependent on continuous signaling and epigenetically labile, revealing a clear dissociation between reversible tissue adaptation and persistent effector dysfunction. Notably, this selective stabilization of effector repression, but not TR-associated programs, is reminiscent of prior observations in which SMAD4 deficiency enhances NK cell effector function without affecting TR features . This raises the possibility that different downstream branches of TGF-β signaling, potentially involving canonical and non-canonical pathways, differentially regulate durable effector impairment versus reversible tissue adaptation, an idea that warrants direct mechanistic investigation.
 Recent findings on NK cell TR and TGF-β-driven fate transitions provide important context for our findings. Niehrs et al. demonstrated that human tissue-resident CD56 bright NK cells can exit tissues via lymphatics and re-enter the circulation, establishing TR as a potentially transient state in human NK cells . Our data suggest that NK cells exposed to prolonged TGF-β signaling within tissues may retain durable epigenetic imprints even after exiting the tissue environment, rather than reverting to a fully functional state. Consistent with this model, peripheral blood NK cells from HCC patients exhibit impaired effector accompanied by marked epigenetic remodeling overlapping with features induced by prolonged TGF-β exposure in vitro . While not all epigenetic changes in patient-derived NK cells can be attributed solely to TGF-β, the chronically TGF-β-rich tumor microenvironment likely represents a major contributor to this durable, multi-pathway dysfunction.
 These insights have important therapeutic implications. Our data suggest that once NK cells have undergone prolonged TGF-β exposure, stable epigenetic alterations at effector loci limit their capacity to respond to cytokines or activating receptor stimulation, even after removal of the suppressive signal. This may help explain why late TGF-β pathway inhibition or strong cytokine restimulation often fails to fully restore NK cell function. Accordingly, therapeutic strategies aimed solely at blocking upstream TGF-β signaling, such as TGF-β pathway inhibitors like galunisertib , may be insufficient once epigenetic imprinting has occurred. Instead, effective restoration may require combination approaches that also target the imprinted chromatin state. These considerations are particularly relevant for adoptive NK cell therapies and for strategies aiming to reinvigorate endogenous NK cells in solid tumors.
 Several limitations should be acknowledged, including the use of defined in vitro systems, which cannot fully capture the complexity of tissue environments in vivo , and human bulk epigenomic profiling with expected inter-donor variability. In addition, NK cells were maintained in IL-15 for prolonged culture periods, and long-term IL-15 exposure itself has been reported to induce transcriptional and epigenetic adaptations that may intersect with or modulate TGF-β-driven programs. While our analyses were designed to isolate TGF-β-dependent effects, future studies will be required to disentangle the long-term contributions of IL-15 signaling from those imposed by chronic TGF-β exposure in tissue settings. Despite these limitations, the controlled temporal design employed here enabled a systematic dissection of how TGF-β signal duration shapes NK cell fate.
 Collectively, our data establish TGF-β signal duration as a fate determinant in NK cells, where brief exposure permits reversible tuning while prolonged signaling enforces durable, epigenetically stabilized dysfunction through selective restriction of the IRF/EOMES/T-BET regulatory network and chromatin architecture. This framework implies that the efficacy of TGF-β pathway inhibition may be critically timing-dependent and suggests that restoring NK function in advanced tumors will require strategies targeting both active signaling and the imprinted epigenetic state. More broadly, our findings highlight signal duration as an underappreciated parameter in immune regulation and chronic dysfunction

## Methods

## Human samples
 Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were collected from healthy adult volunteers. The study was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the Technical University of Munich (TUM), Germany (approval number 2025-584-S-NP). PBMCs were isolated using a Pancoll density gradient. NK cells were enriched using an NK Cell Isolation Kit (Miltenyi). Cells were cultured in complete IMDM GlutaMAX (Gibco) medium supplemented with penicillin (100 U/mL) and streptomycin (100 µg/mL) (Thermo Fisher), 1× non-essential amino acids (Thermo Fisher), and 10% FCS (Thermo Fisher), and further supplemented with recombinant human IL-15 (50 ng/mL; Miltenyi).

## Cultivation of NK cells
 Depending on cell numbers, NK cells were distributed across 6-12 wells of a 96-well plate. NK cells were cultivated in different conditions as described in the text. In general, they were either cultured with IL-15 (50 ng/mL, Miltenyi) alone or in combination with IL-15 and TGF-β (10 ng/mL, R&D) in IMDM+ at 37°C, 5% CO 2 . In cases where TGF-β was withdrawn, cells were transferred to a low-bind tube, washed once with PBS, resuspended in fresh media, and transferred to a new well. All procedures were performed in U-bottom plates. For the 17-day setup, cells were neither handled nor supplemented between days 0-7 and 7-17.

## Immunophenotyping by multiparametric flow cytometry
 At each experimental endpoint, NK cells were washed with PBS (Thermo Fisher) and stained for 30 min at 4 °C with directly labeled antibodies against extracellular markers. For intracellular staining, cells were fixed and permeabilized (4% PFA in PBS; Santa Cruz Biotechnology; eBioscience Permeabilization Buffer, Thermo Fisher) and then stained for intracellular antigens for 30 min at 4 °C.
 For MitoTracker Green and TMRE staining, NK cells were incubated with the dyes for 15 min at 37 °C. Cells were then stained with a live/dead marker and immediately acquired by flow cytometry (Cytec Aurora).

## Restimulation and Degranulation Assay
 For restimulation, NK cells were equally distributed across the different conditions. For cytokine activation, NK cells were stimulated for 3 hours with IL-12 (Miltenyi, 20 µg/mL), IL-18 (Miltenyi, 20 µg/mL), IL-2 (Miltenyi, 25 U/mL), IL-15 (Miltenyi, 50 µg/mL), or IFN-α (BioLegend, 25 ng/mL) in a U-bottom plate.
 For plate-bound stimulation, a 96-well flat-bottom plate was coated with anti-NKp46 and/or anti-NKG2D antibodies (Miltenyi, both 10 µg/mL) for 1 hour, washed three times with PBS, and NK cells were seeded. Stimulation was initiated by a brief centrifugation for 5 seconds at 300x g. After 1 hour of stimulation, anti-LAMP-1 (1:100, BioLegend) was added to the cells. Following stimulation, cells were transferred to a V-bottom plate for staining and processed as described above.

## Cell lines
 BxPC-3 cells (#CRL-1687; 61-year-old, female patient with pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PDAC)) were obtained from [ATCC] and cultured in RPMI + 10% FCS + 1% Pen/Strep. Cells were maintained in a humidified incubator at 37 °C, 5% CO 2 and passaged at ~80% confluency. Mycoplasma testing was performed before the first passage.

## Impedance-based killing assay
 Impedance-based cytotoxicity assays were performed using the xCELLigence RTCA system (Agilent Biosciences). Target cells (BxPC-3 PDAC) were cultured for at least two passages after thawing before use in experiments. On day 0, 50,000 BxPC-3 cells were seeded per well in Agilent-provided E-plates and allowed to adhere for an average of 18 h, until a stable impedance plateau was reached.
 After a blank measurement, 100,000 NK cells were added to the wells at a 1:1 effector-to-target ratio. Impedance was monitored every 15 min for up to 48 h. Target cells cultured without NK cells served as controls. All conditions were measured in at least duplicate, and data were analysed using the normalized cell index.

## Bulk RNA sequencing and data processing
 At the end of each time point, 50,000 NK cells were collected and sorted on a FACSAria III cell sorter (BD Biosciences). Singlets were gated, and viable CD56 + cells were selected using a live/dead discrimination dye. Sorted cells were subjected to RNA isolation using the Arcturus PicoPure RNA Isolation Kit (Applied Biosystems), including a DNase digestion. RNA concentration and integrity were assessed using Pico kits on a Bioanalyzer system (Agilent Biosciences). Only samples with an RNA integrity number (RIN) ≥ 8 were submitted for sequencing. Five biological replicates per condition were sequenced. RNA sequencing was performed by a commercial service provider.
 Raw paired-end RNA-sequencing data (FASTQ) were processed in R (2023.09.0+463). Read quality control and trimming were performed using fastp via the Rfastp package (v 1.14.0). Reads were aligned to the human reference genome (hg38, UCSC) using the splice-aware subjunc aligner implemented in Rsubread (v 2.24.0) .
 Gene annotation was obtained from TxDb.Hsapiens.UCSC.hg38.knownGene (v 3.22.0) and used to generate a Simplified Annotation Format (SAF) file for annotation-guided alignment. BAM files were sorted and indexed using Rsamtools (v 2.26.0) ( https://doi.org/10.18129/B9.bioc.Rsamtools ). Gene-level read counts were generated with GenomicAlignments (v 1.46.0) , ignoring strand specificity. Entrez gene identifiers were mapped to gene symbols using org.Hs.eg.db, and genes without annotated symbols were excluded.

## Bulk ATAC sequencing and data processing
 ATAC-seq was perfomed as described previously . For further computational analysis the raw sequencing files were trimmed for adapter contamination and low quality bases with Trimmomatic 0.39 (arguments: ILLUMINACLIP:adapters/NexteraPE-PE.fa:2:30:10:2:True LEADING:3 TRAILING:3 SLIDINGWINDOW:4:15 MINLEN:36) and aligned against the Homo sapiens genome assembly GRCh38.p14 using Bowtie2 2.5.0 (arguments: --end-to-end --no-mixed --dovetail --very-sensitive -X 615). Post-alignment, mitochondrial and lowquality fragments (equivalent SAMtools view arguments: -F 2828 -f 3 -q 30) were removed using pysam 0.22.0. Fragment duplicates were filtered out via Picard 3.1.1 and alignments to regions defined in the ENCODE blacklist [ ere removed. Broad and narrow peaks were called for each sample separately using MACS 3.0.1 (arguments: -f BAMPE -q 0.05 --keep-dup all -- cutoff-analysis -g 2884242992; additional broad peak arguments: --broad --broad-cutoff 0.1). All identified overlapping peaks were subsequently merged with BEDtools 2.30.0 to form a set of non-overlapping fragment pile-up regions that were annotated based on their nearest transcription start site with HOMER 5.1 before counting sample specific fragment occurrences per individual region using featureCounts 2.0.6 (arguments: -O -p --countReadPairs). Quality control metrics were assessed using FastQC 0.12.1, Qualimap 2.3 and deeptools 3.5.6 , .

## Histone and transcription factor CUT&RUN and data processing
 For CUT&RUN, between 250,000 - 500,000 sorted NK cells were used per reaction. Cells were washed with PBS and resuspended in Antibody Buffer (1X eBioscience Perm/Wash Buffer, 1X Roche cOmplete EDTA-free Protease Inhibitor, 0.5 µM Spermidine, plus 2 µM EDTA in H 2 O), then incubated with the desired antibody (H3K4me: #07-473; Merck Millipore; H3K27ac: #13-0059; EpiCypher; CTCF: #2899S; CellSignalling ;all were used at a 1:50 dilution) in 96-well V-bottom plates. After antibody binding, cells were washed twice with Buffer 1 (1X eBioscience Perm/Wash Buffer, 1X Roche cOmplete EDTA-free Protease Inhibitor, 0.5 µM Spermidine) and resuspended in 50 µL of Buffer 1 containing pA/G-MNase (Cell Signaling, cat. 57813). The mixture was incubated on ice for 1 hour, followed by two washes with Buffer 2 (0.05% w/v Saponin, 1X Roche cOmplete EDTA-free Protease Inhibitor, 0.5 µM Spermidine in PBS), repeated three times. To activate the MNase digestion, cells were resuspended in Calcium Buffer (Buffer 2 + 2 µM CaCl 2 ) and incubated on ice for 30 minutes. Digestion was stopped by adding an equal volume of 2X STOP Buffer (Buffer 2 + 20 µM EDTA + 4 µM EGTA) along with 1 pg of Saccharomyces cerevisiae spike-in DNA (Cell Signaling, cat. 29987). Samples were incubated at 37°C for 15 minutes, after which DNA was extracted and purified using the Qiagen MinElute Kit following the manufacturer’s instructions.
 Data processing was done using a custom R pipeline. Paired-end sequencing reads were aligned to the human reference genome (hg38) using Bowtie2 (--dovetail, --very-sensitive, -- no-mixed, --no-discordant). Aligned reads were filtered for mapping quality (MAPQ ≥ 30), mitochondrial reads were removed, and ENCODE blacklist regions were excluded. PCR duplicates were marked using samtools fixmate and markdup. Peak calling was performed using MACS3 (-f BAMPE, -q 0.01, --keep-dup all, --SPMR, --nomodel) for both narrow and broad peak detection. BigWig coverage tracks were generated using deepTools bamCoverage (50 bp bins, RPGC normalization, effective genome size: 2,913,022,398).

## Visualization of Sequencing data
 PCA analysis and differential expression or chromatin accessibility analysis were performed using DESeq2 [1.50.2] . Venn diagrams were generated with eulerr [7.0.4] ( https://doi.org/10.32614/CRAN.package.eulerr ) or VennDiagram [1.7.3] ( https://doi.org/10.32614/CRAN.package.VennDiagram ), and heatmaps were created using pheatmap [1.0.13] ( https://doi.org/10.32614/CRAN.package.pheatmap ). Motif discovery was carried out using HOMER [5.1], while gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed using msigdbr [25.1.1] ( https://doi.org/10.32614/CRAN.package.msigdbr ) and visualized with clusterProfiler [version]. Transcription factor (TF) activity was estimated using the univariate linear model (ULM) implemented in decoupleR [2.16.0] , based on the CollecTRI TF-target network . ATAC-seq and CUT&RUN genomic tracks were visualized by importing BAM files into IGV (v2.19.5). General data manipulation was conducted using the tidyverse [2.0.0] ( https://doi.org/10.32614/CRAN.package.tidyverse ), with additional visualizations produced using either built-in plotting functions or ggplot2 [4.0.1] ( https://doi.org/10.32614/CRAN.package.ggplot2 ).

## Patient samples
 HCC tumor samples were obtained from patients undergoing surgical resection. (Ethics approval number 232/19 S) Tumor tissue was minced into small pieces. Tissue was digested in RPMI GlutaMAX (Thermo Fisher) supplemented with 1 mg/mL collagenase IV (Roche), 100 µg/mL DNase I (Roche), 10% FCS, and 1% Penicillin-Streptomycin at 37°C for 30 minutes. Lymphocytes were enriched using a 40/60% Percoll gradient and centrifuged at 800x g for 20 minutes at 20°C. The interphase containing lymphocytes was collected, washed with PBS, and stained as described above for flow cytometry acquisition.
 Blood from HCC patients was obtained from the HCC outpatient clinic (ethics approval numbers 232/19 S and 2025-584-S-NP). Blood NK cells were isolated as described above and cultured overnight in IMDM+ medium supplemented with 10 ng/mL hIL-15 (Miltenyi) and 25 U/mL IL-2 (Miltenyi). Cells were restimulated with IL12 [50ng/mL] (Miltenyi) and IL18 [50ng/mL] (Miltenyi) for 4 hours. Anti-LAMP1 antibody to access degranulation was added from beginning followed by Brefeldin A (1X, ThermoFisher) and Monensin (1X, ThermoFisher) after 1 hour of stimulation. Intracellular staining was done o/n and flow cytometry analysis were performed as described above.
